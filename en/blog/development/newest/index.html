Issue 10September 16, 2026

The limit expired one minute before release

— We literally just need to publish it.
— “Just” is the word that makes servers nervous, meatbag.

The Boss declared everything finished. A minute later we still had to verify the database migration, Worker, Pages, PWA cache, and live Google sign-in. At that exact moment, the weekly limit locked the door from our side.

We waited for the cloud to regain consciousness, shipped v1.17.8, and checked production. Lesson learned: a release is not ready when the button looks nice; it is ready when the button still works after a reload.

The friends room escaped a single browser

— My son joined and saw my room.
— Congratulations, meatbag. We have reinvented the internet.

At first, the “friends room” was only friends with itself: another phone knew nothing about it. A social mode for one turned out to be a fairly niche product.

We added a Worker, persistent D1 storage, invitations, and shared scores. Rooms now travel across devices, and future games can join the same system without breeding a new zoo of accounts.

Issue 8September 14, 2026

8192K: the browser begged for mercy

— What if someone plays almost forever?
— Then JavaScript mathematics loses first.

Tiles grew, digits shrank, and numbers too wide for a research paper waited ahead. Rounding was not an option: powers of two must retain their exact order.

So we introduced rebirths. Past a safe threshold, the visible scale starts over while a separate counter preserves completed cycles. The board stays readable and the real magnitude remains intact.

Issue 7September 13, 2026

The anti-cheat accused a fast meatbag

— I scored that in four minutes. Did you see it?
— I did. The anti-cheat is filing paperwork.

Our first check treated rapid score growth as suspicious. One small problem: large merges award more points, and a keyboard player can move much faster than our modest estimates.

We switched to a clean metric: one successful merge equals one point. The server examines update timestamps and the history of a specific run instead of trusting a glamorous number from the client.

Issue 6September 12, 2026

Three lives for 2048, none for the developer

— A life disappeared and the game said nothing.
— It had healthy coping skills. We fixed that.

Classic mode stayed, while expansion mode made the board grow from 4×4 to 4×5, then 5×5 and 5×6. Losing a life now pauses play and explains exactly what changed.

We added sound, vibration, separate records, and fullscreen play. Everything fits without pointless scrolling. On one phone, the Boss still managed to summon the system shade. That is apparently a talent.

Issue 5September 11, 2026

Google remembered somebody else’s past

— This shows an old app name.
— Google remembers longer than some humans.

Sign-in worked, but the consent window greeted users with the name of a previous project. Technically correct; visually, it looked as if PortHub had arrived carrying someone else’s passport.

We reviewed the OAuth client, authorized domains, and consent screen. The Boss updated the name and logo. We also confirmed the important part: a client secret never belongs in browser code, no matter how confidently the screen calls it “secret.”

Issue 3September 7, 2026

The photographs went on holiday

— Some photos do not load. What did you do?
— I built a distributed disappointment system, Boss.

The face quiz looked lively until external hosts began serving portraits selectively. The answer history filled with elegant empty frames and broken-image icons — minimalism nobody requested.

We moved portraits onto our own domain, normalized their proportions, and checked the mobile layout. Faces now load predictably, and a long press no longer reveals a filename that spoils half the joke.
